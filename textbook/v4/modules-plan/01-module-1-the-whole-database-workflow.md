# Module 1: The Whole Database Workflow

### Purpose

- Give students a simple end-to-end view of the database process before they study isolated skills in depth.
- Establish early that a polished artifact is not the same thing as a justified database decision.

### Objectives

- Describe the overall workflow from business process to working database.
- Distinguish the ER Diagram from the Database Design Diagram.
- Explain where SQL, normalization, implementation, and administration fit in the whole system.
- Judge when a scenario calls for a database versus a simpler tool.

### Human Must Know

- how to identify the business need behind the system
- what each workflow stage is for
- why the ERD and DBDD are different artifacts
- when a database is appropriate

### AI May Assist With

- generating examples and terminology practice
- summarizing workflow stages
- producing first-pass scenario classifications

### Christian Integration Focus

- connect database choice to truthful, neighbor-serving business practice
- frame poor structure as a stewardship, trust, and follow-through problem rather than only an efficiency problem

### Integration Touchpoints

- use at least one scenario prompt that asks who could be harmed when poor data structure distorts reporting or service
- include at least one project checkpoint asking how the chosen workflow helps the organization tell the truth about its operations

### Lessons

#### Lesson 1.1: Why Databases Matter

- Compare spreadsheet, disconnected-file, and database approaches.
- Distinguish business process, data, information, and database.
- Judge when a database is warranted.

#### Lesson 1.2: From Business Process to Database

- Walk through the course workflow from need to implementation.
- Match artifacts to workflow stages.
- Explain what the ERD shows and what the DBDD adds.

### Assessment Strategy

Standalone tool-choice writeups are weak evidence in an AI-available course. This module should therefore use timed and classification-based assessment rather than polished essays.

### Primary Graded Assessment

#### Case Framing Check

- Format: timed quiz plus short written justification
- Students classify a short case, identify tracked information, place workflow stages in order, and distinguish ERD from DBDD

### Secondary Evidence

- short artifact-boundary classification exercise
- one annotated scenario explanation

### What To Grade

- correctness of scenario classification
- ability to identify must-track information
- accuracy of workflow reasoning
- correct distinction between conceptual and implementation-ready artifacts
- ability to name a realistic trust, stewardship, or accountability implication when a case naturally calls for it

### Module Assessment Tasks

- decide whether a scenario needs a database or a simpler tool
- identify the information the system must track
- place workflow stages in correct order
- classify which details belong in an ERD and which belong in a DBDD

### Why This Assessment Holds Up Better

- it uses short, case-specific prompts
- it rewards correct framing over polished prose
- it checks whether students understand what counts as the right next step
