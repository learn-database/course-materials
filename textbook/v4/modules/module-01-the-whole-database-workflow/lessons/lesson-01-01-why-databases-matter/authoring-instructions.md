## Purpose Of This Lesson

Open Module 1 by teaching students why structured data support exists before they study workflow stages, diagrams, or SQL Server implementation. Keep the lesson beginner-friendly, business-facing, and focused on tool-fit judgment rather than technical enthusiasm.

## Lesson Role Inside Module 1

- This is the opening lesson for the whole course workflow.
- It establishes that a polished artifact is not the same thing as justified database reasoning.
- It prepares Lesson `1.2`, which will move from business need into the end-to-end workflow and artifact boundaries.
- It supports the module's case-framing assessment by giving students the vocabulary and judgment needed to decide whether a database is warranted.

## Required Emphases

- Keep the lesson business-first and conceptual.
- Teach the distinction among `business process`, `data`, `information`, and `database`.
- Clearly compare `spreadsheet`, `disconnected-file`, and `database` approaches.
- Teach when a database is warranted and when it is unnecessary overhead.
- Connect poor structure to trust, stewardship, truthful reporting, and follow-through.
- Keep Christian integration embedded in normal lesson elements rather than in a separate devotional section.

## Required Content Moves

The student draft should:

- explain why organizations need reliable records for recurring work
- distinguish the three tool approaches using realistic business examples and non-examples
- include at least one simple comparison table or structured contrast
- include at least one scenario where a spreadsheet is sufficient
- include at least one scenario where disconnected files create reporting or service problems
- include at least one scenario where a database is the stronger fit
- include at least one practice or assessment element asking whether a database is actually warranted
- include at least one embedded touchpoint asking who could be harmed when poor structure distorts reporting or service
- include an AI-aware verification or critique element so students must judge reasoning instead of only produce a polished answer

The instructor draft should:

- explain how the lesson supports the module's timed and classification-based assessment pattern
- name the primary and secondary learning types from the v4 strategy map
- keep grading aligned to terminology accuracy, scenario classification, and business justification
- explain how the lesson avoids over-relying on AI-generable artifact production

## Boundaries

- Do not drift into ERDs, DBDDs, keys, foreign keys, normalization, or SQL syntax.
- `table`, `row`, and `column` may appear only as early recognition vocabulary.
- Do not imply that a database is always superior to a spreadsheet.
- Do not make "use AI to build a database" the point of the lesson.
- Do not turn the lesson into a generic ethics lecture divorced from database choices.

## Assessment Expectations

Use the Module 1 assessment pattern:

- short classification or case-check work
- brief written justification
- evidence based on explanation, comparison, and judgment

At least one graded element should ask students to:

- decide whether a database is warranted
- justify the choice with business reasoning
- identify a trust, stewardship, or truthful-reporting consequence when poor structure matters
